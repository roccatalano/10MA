1J.1
I can solve simultaneous equations by adding or subtracting them.
Short answer · 1 mk

Solve $x + y = 15$ and $x - y = 7$ using the elimination method.

add: $2x = 22$, $x = 11$
$11 + y = 15$, $y = 4$
Cambridge Essential Mathematics 9 (3rd Ed), Ch 2 review, short answer Q14d (p. 165); adapted: stem reworded for a single part
Multiple choice · 1 mk

Consider the two equations: $2x - y + 3 = 0$ ① and $4x - y + 5 = 0$ ②. If equation ② is subtracted from equation ①, the resulting equation will be:

  1. A $-2x - 2 = 0$
  2. B $-2x - 8 = 0$
  3. C $2x + 2 = 0$
  4. D $2x + 8 = 0$
  5. E $2x - 2 = 0$
① − ②: $-2x - 2 = 0$ (A)
Oxford Maths 10 VC (2022), Ch 4 review, multiple-choice Q15 (p. 207)
Short answer · 2 mk

Use the elimination method to solve $x + 5y = 13$ and $x + 2y = 4$.

subtract: $3y = 9$, $y = 3$
$x + 6 = 4$, $x = -2$
$x = -2$, $y = 3$
Oxford Maths 10 VC (2022), Ch 4 review, short answer Q16b (p. 208); adapted: stem reworded for a single part
1J.2
I can use the elimination method to solve simultaneous equations.
Short answer · 2 mk

Solve $3x + 2y = -19$ and $4x - y = -7$ using the elimination method.

$\times 2$: $8x - 2y = -14$
add: $11x = -33$, $x = -3$
$4(-3) - y = -7$, $y = -5$
Cambridge Essential Mathematics 9 (3rd Ed), Ch 2 review, short answer Q14e (p. 165); adapted: stem reworded for a single part
Short answer · 2 mk

Use the elimination method to solve $2x - y = 7$ and $3x + 5y = 4$.

$\times 5$: $10x - 5y = 35$
add: $13x = 39$, $x = 3$
$6 - y = 7$, $y = -1$
Oxford Maths 10 VC (2022), Ch 4 review, short answer Q16c (p. 208); adapted: stem reworded for a single part
Short answer · 2 mk

Solve $3x - 5y = 7$ and $5x + 2y = 22$ using the elimination method.

$\times 2$: $6x - 10y = 14$; $\times 5$: $25x + 10y = 110$
add: $31x = 124$, $x = 4$
$12 - 5y = 7$, $y = 1$
Cambridge Essential Mathematics 9 (3rd Ed), Ch 2 review, short answer Q14f (p. 165); adapted: stem reworded for a single part
