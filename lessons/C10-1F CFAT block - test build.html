1F.1
I can find the gradient of a line joining two points.
Multiple choice · 1 mk

The gradient of the line joining the points $(1, -2)$ and $(5, 6)$ is:

  1. A $2$
  2. B $1$
  3. C $3$
  4. D $-1$
  5. E $\dfrac{1}{3}$
$m = \dfrac{6 - (-2)}{5 - 1} = \dfrac{8}{4} = 2$ (A)
Cambridge Essential Mathematics 9 (3rd Ed), Ch 4 review, multiple-choice Q3 (p. 323)
Multiple choice · 1 mk

The gradient of the line passing through the points $(1, 8)$ and $(-3, -6)$ is:

  1. A $1$
  2. B $-1$
  3. C $\dfrac{2}{7}$
  4. D $\dfrac{7}{2}$
  5. E $-\dfrac{7}{2}$
$m = \dfrac{-6 - 8}{-3 - 1} = \dfrac{-14}{-4} = \dfrac{7}{2}$ (D)
Oxford Maths 10 VC (2022), Ch 4 review, multiple-choice Q8 (p. 207)
Short answer · 2 mk

Find the missing coordinate, $n$, if the line joining $(-1, 3)$ and $(2, n)$ has gradient $2$.

$\dfrac{n - 3}{2 - (-1)} = 2$
$n - 3 = 6$
$n = 9$
Cambridge Essential Mathematics 9 (3rd Ed), Ch 4 review, short answer Q11a (p. 322); adapted: stem reworded for a single part
1F.2
I can find the equation of a line using a point and the $y$-intercept.
Short answer · 1 mk

Give the equation of the straight line that has gradient $3$ and passes through the point $(0, 2)$.

$m = 3$, $c = 2$
$y = 3x + 2$
Cambridge Essential Mathematics 9 (3rd Ed), Ch 4 review, short answer Q8a (p. 321); adapted: stem reworded for a single part
Multiple choice · 1 mk

A straight line has a gradient of $-2$ and passes through the point $(0, 5)$. Its equation is:

  1. A $2y = -2x + 10$
  2. B $y = -2x + 5$
  3. C $y = 5x - 2$
  4. D $y - 2x = 5$
  5. E $y = -2(x - 5)$
$m = -2$, $c = 5$
$y = -2x + 5$ (B)
Cambridge Essential Mathematics 9 (3rd Ed), Ch 4 review, multiple-choice Q6 (p. 324)
Multiple choice · 1 mk

The equation for the linear graph with a gradient of $\dfrac{3}{4}$ and a $y$-intercept of $\dfrac{5}{8}$ is:

  1. A $8x - 6y + 5 = 0$
  2. B $8y = 6x - 5$
  3. C $6x - 8y + 5 = 0$
  4. D $6y = 8x + 5$
  5. E $6x - 5y + 8 = 0$
$y = \dfrac{3}{4}x + \dfrac{5}{8}$, $\times 8$: $8y = 6x + 5$
$6x - 8y + 5 = 0$ (C)
Oxford Maths 10 VC (2022), Ch 4 review, multiple-choice Q7 (p. 207)
1F.3
I can find the equation of a line given two points.
Short answer · 1 mk

Give the equation of the straight line that has gradient $\dfrac{4}{3}$ and passes through the point $(6, 3)$.

$3 = \dfrac{4}{3}(6) + c$
$3 = 8 + c$, $c = -5$
$y = \dfrac{4}{3}x - 5$
Cambridge Essential Mathematics 9 (3rd Ed), Ch 4 review, short answer Q8c (p. 321); adapted: stem reworded for a single part
Short answer · 1 mk

Find the equation of the line that passes through the points $(1, 3)$ and $(2, 4)$. Write your answer in the form $y = mx + c$.

$m = \dfrac{4 - 3}{2 - 1} = 1$
$3 = 1 + c$, $c = 2$
$y = x + 2$
Oxford Maths 10 VC (2022), Ch 4 review, short answer Q11a (p. 208); adapted: stem reworded for a single part
Short answer · 2 mk

Find the equation of the line that passes through the points $(-1, 5)$ and $(3, -7)$. Write your answer in the form $y = mx + c$.

$m = \dfrac{-7 - 5}{3 - (-1)} = -3$
$5 = -3(-1) + c$, $c = 2$
$y = -3x + 2$
Oxford Maths 10 VC (2022), Ch 4 review, short answer Q11b (p. 208); adapted: stem reworded for a single part
