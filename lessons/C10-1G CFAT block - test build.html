1G.1
I can find the distance between two points.
Multiple choice · 1 mk

The length of the line segment joining the points $A(1, 2)$ and $B(4, 6)$ is:

  1. A $5$
  2. B $\dfrac{4}{3}$
  3. C $7$
  4. D $\sqrt{5}$
  5. E $\sqrt{2}$
$d = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = 5$ (A)
Cambridge Essential Mathematics 9 (3rd Ed), Ch 4 review, multiple-choice Q8 (p. 324)
Short answer · 1 mk

For the line segment joining $(2, 4)$ and $(6, 8)$, find the length (to two decimal places where applicable).

$d = \sqrt{(6 - 2)^2 + (8 - 4)^2}$
$= \sqrt{32} \approx 5.66$
Cambridge Essential Mathematics 9 (3rd Ed), Ch 4 review, short answer Q10a ii (p. 322); adapted: stem reworded for a single part
Short answer · 2 mk

For the line segment joining $(-5, 7)$ and $(-1, -2)$, find the length (to two decimal places where applicable).

$d = \sqrt{(-1 - (-5))^2 + (-2 - 7)^2}$
$= \sqrt{16 + 81} = \sqrt{97} \approx 9.85$
Cambridge Essential Mathematics 9 (3rd Ed), Ch 4 review, short answer Q10d ii (p. 322); adapted: stem reworded for a single part
1G.2
I can find the midpoint of a line segment joining two points.
Short answer · 1 mk

For the line segment joining $(2, 4)$ and $(6, 8)$, find the midpoint.

$M = \left(\dfrac{2 + 6}{2}, \dfrac{4 + 8}{2}\right)$
$= (4, 6)$
Cambridge Essential Mathematics 9 (3rd Ed), Ch 4 review, short answer Q10a i (p. 322); adapted: stem reworded for a single part
Short answer · 1 mk

For the line segment joining $(-2, 1)$ and $(2, 7)$, find the midpoint.

$M = \left(\dfrac{-2 + 2}{2}, \dfrac{1 + 7}{2}\right)$
$= (0, 4)$
Cambridge Essential Mathematics 9 (3rd Ed), Ch 4 review, short answer Q10c i (p. 322); adapted: stem reworded for a single part
Short answer · 2 mk

Find the missing coordinate, $n$, if the midpoint of the line segment joining $(n, 1)$ and $(1, -3)$ is $(3.5, -1)$.

$\dfrac{n + 1}{2} = 3.5$
$n + 1 = 7$
$n = 6$
Cambridge Essential Mathematics 9 (3rd Ed), Ch 4 review, short answer Q11c (p. 322); adapted: stem reworded for a single part
1G.3
I can use a given distance to find coordinates.
Short answer · 2 mk

Find the missing coordinate, $n$, if the line segment joining $(-2, 2)$ and $(4, n)$ has length $10$, $n > 0$.

$10^2 = 6^2 + (n - 2)^2$
$(n - 2)^2 = 64$, $n - 2 = \pm 8$
$n = 10$ (as $n > 0$)
Cambridge Essential Mathematics 9 (3rd Ed), Ch 4 review, short answer Q11b (p. 322); adapted: stem reworded for a single part
Short answer · 2 mk

The following three points are plotted on a Cartesian plane: $A(-2, 3)$, $B(1, 12)$ and $C(7, 10)$. Show that line segments drawn between these points form a right-angled triangle with the points $A$, $B$ and $C$ as vertices.

$AB^2 = 3^2 + 9^2 = 90$, $BC^2 = 6^2 + 2^2 = 40$
$AC^2 = 9^2 + 7^2 = 130$
$90 + 40 = 130$, so right-angled at $B$
Oxford Maths 10 VC (2022), Exercise 4E Q20a (p. 196)
Short answer · 2 mk

Prove that quadrilateral $KLMN$, with vertices at $K(0, 0)$, $L(2, 4)$, $M(6, 2)$ and $N(4, -2)$, is a square. Then find its perimeter correct to one decimal place.

$KL = LM = MN = NK = \sqrt{20}$
diagonals $KM = LN = \sqrt{40}$, so a square
perimeter $= 4\sqrt{20} \approx 17.9$
Oxford Maths 10 VC (2022), Exercise 4E Q22 (p. 196)
