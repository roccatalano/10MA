Question 2 9A.2 · L0   1 mark
A survey is conducted to determine how many siblings children have. Which of the following methods of conducting the survey is more likely to lead to bias?
Method 1: Choosing $20$ children at random from a school.
Method 2: Asking $20$ children waiting outside a primary school at the end of a day.
Method 2: children waiting outside a primary school at the end of the day are more likely to be picking up a younger brother or sister, so they are more likely to have siblings.

Question 4 9B.2 · L0   3 marks
The ages of boys in an after-school athletics squad are shown in the table below.
AgeFrequency
103
118
1212
134
143
a  State the total number of boys in the squad.
b  Calculate the mean age of the squad, correct to two decimal places.
c  What is the median age of the boys in the squad?
a $3 + 8 + 12 + 4 + 3 = 30$ boys
b $\dfrac{10 \times 3 + 11 \times 8 + 12 \times 12 + 13 \times 4 + 14 \times 3}{30} = \dfrac{356}{30} = 11.87$ years
c The $15$th and $16$th ages are both $12$, so the median is $12$ years.
